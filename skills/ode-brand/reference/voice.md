# Ode with Anthropic — voice and writing standard

The external-prose rules for any client- or partner-facing artifact. Source: the
Ode Brand Hub "Brand/Verbal Identity & Tone" page (and its full guideline PDF).
This file carries the load-bearing rules so on-brand artifacts read right by
default.

## The voice: constant attributes, tone shifts by context

Our voice stays constant; our tone shifts by context. Four core attributes:

- **Ambitious** — expansive, confident, bold. Use when stating our POV or
  describing impact; dial back when teaching first-timers or acknowledging
  tradeoffs.
- **Exacting** — precise, diligent, methodical. Use when explaining specifics,
  workflows, or docs; dial back for vision/inspiration moments.
- **Kinetic** — hopeful, candid, open. Use when introducing Ode or painting the
  bigger picture; dial back for technical/compliance content.
- **Grounded** — warm, clear, honest. Use for headlines and trust-building; dial
  back when precision can't be compromised.

## Naming

- The brand is **Ode**, and the endorsed form is **Ode with Anthropic** — the
  default logo lockup carries the "with Anthropic" endorsement. Use "Ode with
  Anthropic" where the relationship matters (first mention, titles, signatures);
  "Ode" is fine thereafter.
- Say "we" or "Ode", never "the Respondent" or similar third-person self-reference,
  even when paraphrasing RFP language.
- Ode is the new name for the team formerly branded Fractional AI. Don't mix the old
  name into new external artifacts.

## Hard rules

- **US English, always.** finalize, organize, optimize, prioritize, analyze, color,
  behavior, defense, license, center, program, catalog, modeling, canceled. Applies
  to prose, captions, code comments, file names.
- **Never use em-dashes (—).** Replace with a colon, semicolon, comma, period, or
  parentheses, chosen to preserve meaning. En-dashes for numeric/date ranges
  ("10–20", "Aug–Sep 2026") are fine.
- **Titles label, they don't pitch.** The conclusion belongs in the first sentence
  of the lede, never the headline. Section titles read like table-of-contents
  entries, not slide headlines. (The approved brand headlines above are the
  exception — they are campaign lines, not section titles.)

## AI-writing tells to remove

- No significance inflation: "pivotal", "testament", "evolving landscape", "vital
  role", "groundbreaking".
- No promotional filler: "vibrant", "nestled", "breathtaking", "showcasing", "rich
  heritage".
- No superficial -ing tack-ons: "highlighting…", "underscoring…", "ensuring…",
  "reflecting…".
- No vague attributions: "experts argue", "industry observers note" — cite specifics
  or cut.
- No copula avoidance: say "is" / "are", not "serves as", "stands as", "boasts".
- No sycophantic artifacts: "Great question!", "I hope this helps!", "Let me know
  if…".
- No formulaic structures: rule-of-three lists, false ranges ("from X to Y, from A
  to B"), "Challenges and Future Prospects" sections, negative parallelisms ("It's
  not just X; it's Y").
- **No manufactured juxtapositions.** Don't frame a claim as "X, not Y" (or "rather
  than Y", "instead of Y") unless Y was genuinely on the table as a real
  alternative. Say X directly.
- No filler phrases: "In order to", "It is important to note that", "Due to the fact
  that".
- No excessive hedging: "could potentially possibly be argued that…might".
- No generic positive conclusions: "The future looks bright", "Exciting times lie
  ahead".
- No signposting: "Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know" — just say the
  thing.
- No persuasive-authority tropes: "At its core", "The real question is", "What
  really matters".
- No emoji decorations, no mechanical boldface, no inline-header bullet lists.

## Positive style
- Vary sentence length and structure. Have opinions. Be specific. Use "is" / "are" /
  "has" freely.
- Compress to the load-bearing claim. If a reason fits in one sentence, don't make
  it three paragraphs. Detail belongs in an appendix or callout; the main flow
  carries the claim.
- After drafting, do a final anti-AI pass: ask "what makes this obviously
  AI-generated?", fix the tells, then finalize.

## Never assert external-system behavior from memory
Any claim about a third-party tool's behavior, feature, integration, pricing, or API
must be backed by current documentation fetched in-session. If you can't find the
docs, say so and mark the claim unverified rather than guessing. This applies even to
well-known products.
